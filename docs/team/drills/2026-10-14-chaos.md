# Chaos drill, 2026-10-14

Seven faults, each injected into the running full-stack rehearsal
([record](2026-10-14-full-stack.md)) by a script under `deploy/chaos/`, each asserting what
OPERATIONS.md says should happen. One pass of the whole suite at version `2bc1fb8f1c3f`
(`deploy/chaos/run.sh`, about 5.5 minutes of scripts): seven of seven pass. Where a first run failed,
the table says what it showed and what was changed.

| #   | Fault                                                   | Script                               | Result  | Time |
| --- | ------------------------------------------------------- | ------------------------------------ | ------- | ---- |
| 1   | Provider answers 503 to every call                      | `provider-5xx.mjs`                   | 11 / 11 | 51 s |
| 2   | Provider accepts the call and never answers             | `provider-hang.mjs`                  | 7 / 7   | 10 s |
| 3   | Postgres restarted under a running interview and a job  | `postgres-restart.mjs`               | 14 / 14 | 16 s |
| 4   | Worker killed with SIGKILL in the middle of an analysis | `kill-worker.mjs`                    | 11 / 11 | 97 s |
| 5   | API killed with SIGKILL in the middle of an SSE turn    | `kill-api.mjs`                       | 33 / 33 | 94 s |
| 6   | Caddy restarted with a turn in flight                   | `caddy-restart.mjs`                  | 8 / 8   | 12 s |
| 7   | The uploads volume and the backups volume nearly full   | `disk-full.mjs` (two tmpfs overlays) | 17 / 17 | 46 s |

A second pass was made after the security and specification-quality work was merged
(`f2e436f6c20f`, a first deployment on a fresh scratch directory). Five scripts passed first time.
`postgres-restart` did not, and `kill-worker` was blocked behind its leftover: in that run the
analysis job had been fetched in the very instant Postgres went down. The queue had marked it
`active`, the response never reached the worker (no worker log line names the job), and it sat there
with a healthy worker until its time limit (an hour for `analysis.run`). On the first pass and on the
rerun the same script finished the analysis on its own in seconds, so the outcome depends on a race
the script cannot control. It is not a lost job and not a doubled one, but it is the same slow
recovery as a killed worker (F2); the script now records which of the two happened and, in the slow
case, simulates the expiry as `kill-worker` does. Rerun: `postgres-restart` 14 / 14 (finished on its
own), `kill-worker` 11 / 11.

What each one asserts, and what it measured:

1. **Provider 5xx** (`FAKE_ERROR_STATUS=503`, rate 1). The turn ends with `provider_error` in 446 ms
   (one retry), saying the answer is saved; the session stays `active`; the answer is in the
   transcript; after five failures inside a minute the circuit opens and a turn fails in 27 to 29 ms
   instead of 460 to 520; the health sweep raised `provider.error_rate` on the webhook; when the
   provider returned, the same session carried on.
2. **Provider hang.** Before the fix, the turn ended at 30 s, the ceiling (`PROVIDER_TIMEOUT_MS`),
   not at the 8 s first-token deadline that was configured: the deadline lived in the stream guard,
   which starts once headers exist. After the fix the turn ends at the deadline; the turn lock is
   free at once (the next turn was accepted 1.1 s after the provider came back); the hung answer is
   kept.
3. **Postgres restart.** Readiness reports `database:false` / 503 while it is down and returns by
   itself 2.7 s after the restart began, every service is healthy again at 7.1 s, with no manual
   action. A turn taken in the gap fails in 7 ms with `internal` (the web app words the code, and
   keeps the draft); afterwards the session resumes. The analysis queued before the restart
   finished exactly once (one `completed` row for its id, 51 job rows before and after, none failed);
   no `api.down` alert for an outage this short.
4. **Worker kill -9 mid-job.** The job stays `active` on the queue, the restart policy returns the
   worker healthy in 7 s. The job is **not** retried until its `expireInSeconds` (3600 for
   `analysis.run`): twenty seconds later it is still `active`, the analysis still `running`, and
   "Run analysis" answers conflict. With the passage of that time simulated (the job's start moved
   back two hours: labelled as a simulation in the script), the queue retried it, it completed once,
   one analysis row, one opportunity per cluster (5 for 5). This is the "returns to retry after
   `expireInSeconds`" row of the plan, true and slow; see finding F2 in the full-stack record, and
   the recovery step now in OPERATIONS.md (job.stuck).
5. **API kill -9 mid-stream.** The client's stream ends with a socket error 2.9 s in; Docker brings
   the API back healthy 7 s after the kill; the participant's answer was saved before the cut and
   exists exactly once; the session is intact. Turns are refused with `conflict` for 82 s (the turn
   lock's 90 s TTL, held by a process that no longer exists), then accepted; the transcript only
   grew (6 to 9 messages), nothing doubled. See F3.
6. **Caddy restart.** The in-flight stream is cut, the proxy answers again in 1.9 s, with the same
   certificate (the local CA persists in `caddy_data`), healthy again by itself, and the participant's
   session carries on.
7. **Disk nearly full.** The uploads volume became a 3 MB tmpfs filled to 97 %: an upload that does
   not fit fails in 31 ms with `500 internal` (finding F4: honest but unspecific), the interview
   carries on in text, the health sweep raised `disk.high` **critical** ("Disk 96% used") on the
   webhook. The backups volume became a 100 KB tmpfs: `backup.sh once` exits 1 with age's own
   "no space left on device", no `.part` file is left, the status file says failed with a reason,
   `backup-health.sh` reports unhealthy, the sweep raised `backup.failed`; with the real volume
   back, one backup restored the status.

## What the first runs showed (fixed in the scripts or the product)

| What failed                                                                    | Cause                                                                                                                           | Change                                                                                         |
| ------------------------------------------------------------------------------ | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | ---------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| The worker stayed down after `docker kill`                                     | Docker treats `docker kill` as a manual stop; the restart policy does not apply. It is not what a crash looks like              | `crash()` in `deploy/chaos/lib.mjs` signals from the host PID namespace (privileged one-shot)  |
| Provider hang ended at 30 s, not the first-token deadline                      | Product defect (see 2 above)                                                                                                    | `headersWithinMs` in the OpenAI-compatible adapter, with unit tests                            |
| "Backup unhealthy" and the `backup.failed` alert were not seen within a minute | Docker's own health needs three failed 60 s checks, and `backup.failed` is raised by the platform's health sweep, not by a file | The script asks `backup-health.sh` directly and runs the sweep on demand (`pgboss.job` insert) |
| The backup failure reason said "pg_dump failed" when age could not write       | One message for a failing pipeline of two tools                                                                                 | Names both, and a full disk, and points at the container log                                   |
| A synchronous restart of Caddy stopped the script from reading its own stream  | The script, not the product                                                                                                     | The restart is spawned asynchronously                                                          |

## What this does not show

- It is one process of each kind on one host: no failover exists and none is claimed.
- The 7-second and 11-second recoveries are on a warm machine with images present.
- `kill-worker.mjs` simulates the passage of `expireInSeconds`; the hour itself was not waited out.
- TLS renewal blocked, a missing backup at 26 h and the other alert kinds were not provoked here;
  they have their own harness tests (`deploy/test/notify.test.sh`, `packages/core/test`).
- The provider fallback route (`provider.fallback_active`) was not configured in this stack, so
  "the interview continues on the fallback" is covered by `breaker-fallback.test.ts` and not here.
- Voice under fault (provider realtime socket refused or dropped) is covered by unit tests of the
  degradation chain, not by a script.
