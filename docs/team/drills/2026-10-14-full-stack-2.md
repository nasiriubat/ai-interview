## Is legitimate traffic wrongly throttled

The load now has a measure for it: `legit_throttled` counts a 429 on a link landing or open, and
`full` and `office` fail a threshold at zero.

| Profile                                                                | Limits                   | Refused                                                  |
| ---------------------------------------------------------------------- | ------------------------ | -------------------------------------------------------- |
| Launch burst of 100 plus 40 concurrent interviews, all on one campaign | 120 an hour, 500 tenant  | 311 of 499 open attempts (retries after 5 s counted)     |
| The same, arrivals spread over three campaigns                         | 120 an hour, 500 tenant  | 0 of 277 (no margin: 113 of 120 on the busiest campaign) |
| 40 people behind one address over two minutes                          | 30 a minute a route      | 0                                                        |
| 60 people behind one address in a minute                               | 30 a minute a route      | 3 people                                                 |
| 90 people behind one address in a minute                               | 30 a minute a route      | 60 of 90                                                 |
| 90 people behind one address in a minute                               | 120 a minute a route     | 0                                                        |
| Everything on one campaign, 279 opens in five minutes                  | 300 an hour, 1000 tenant | 0                                                        |

Reading it honestly. The 311 includes the generator's own retries (a refused virtual user waits 5
s and opens again) and the first run's burst reached only one campaign because of a harness fault
(defect 5), so it is a concentrated launch by accident: not a forecast of a real count. What it
does show is real: a campaign announced to a company takes more than 120 people inside an hour,
and the cap is a wall, not a slope. Behind one office address, 30 a minute on each of the two link
routes is thirty people a minute, which an announcement in a chat or a room of 90 exceeds at once.
Decisions: the per-address bucket 120 a minute (`RATE_LIMIT_PUBLIC_MAX`, D-310) and 300 and 1000
for the campaign and tenant hour (D-311). The campaign total (2000) was left: it is the leak
ceiling, and a campaign inviting more people sets it. These numbers were produced by a generator
that needs one source address per virtual user; a CGNAT mobile carrier shares more addresses than
an office does, so 120 a minute is still a judgement, not a measurement of a mobile launch.

## Storage quota, task 2

There is no S3 adapter in the repository; the object store is a directory or memory behind
`StoragePort`. What exists, and what was done, is the classification: `isOutOfSpace` now treats
the quota errors an S3-compatible store gives (`QuotaExceeded`, `StorageQuotaExceeded`,
`storage_cap_exceeded`, `InsufficientStorage`, HTTP 507, and the same through `cause`) like `ENOSPC`
and `EDQUOT`, so they become `StorageFull` and `storage_full` 507 through the code that already
exists (D-313; 16 cases in `packages/core/test/storage.test.ts`). A throttle (`SlowDown`) and
`AccessDenied` are not mistaken for it. **Not done**: an S3 adapter that calls it, and a run
against a real bucket.

## What was not run

- No real model provider, no real SMTP relay, no Let's Encrypt (Caddy's local CA), no offsite
  copy (`RCLONE_REMOTE` empty), so no restore of an offsite copy on another machine.
- The on-host Whisper model was not loaded; embeddings answer 404, so analysis clustered lexically
  (`embedding: lexical`) and the pgvector path was not exercised.
- Not run: Playwright journeys against the stack, a phone, a real network, a CGNAT mobile address
  pool, a database larger than a few thousand rows, more than one tenant.
- `upgrade.sh` with the pre-upgrade backup was exercised by hand in its pieces (the backup, the
  drill and the restore) and refused correctly once on the restart-looping backup; the second
  upgrade used `--skip-backup` because the stack held no data worth a dump, so the full
  pre-upgrade-backup path with a non-root `backup` was not run as one command.
- `ops-notify` as uid 10001 was observed healthy and delivering (`provider.error_rate`, `disk.high`
  and `backup.failed` webhooks arrived in the chaos run); moving an alert file written by the
  application's uid was exercised by those, not tested in isolation.
