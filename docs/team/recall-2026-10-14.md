# Requirement recall and a live re-run, 2026-10-14

The second pass of `docs/team/spec-quality-2026-10.md` ended with a question it could not answer:
fresh interviews record fewer requirements (median 7 for the ward-notes persona against a target of
8 to 20) and the share of stated capabilities no requirement covers did not fall (39%). Either real
requirements were being lost by dedupe, folding and read-back withdrawals, or the earlier lists were
padded with restatements. This note settles it with a measurement that does not let one model both
say what the owner stated and judge whether it was covered, then re-runs the live harness on the
merged tip. Decisions are D-300 to D-305 in `docs/discovery/decisions.md`.

Short answer: **requirements are not being lost in any amount the measurement can see; the earlier
lists were padded.** The live re-run found two defects the quality work had not: duplicated requirement
ids in a finished specification, and a cluster losing its opportunity to a 1,300 character rationale.
Both are fixed here with tests.

## The measurement

`evals/live/recall.live.ts`, with its deterministic half and 16 unit tests in `evals/src/recall.ts`.

1. **Stated capabilities, independent pass.** For each saved interview a model that is none of the
   pipeline's (`google/gemini-2.5-flash-lite`; the pipeline uses haiku, mistral-small and gpt-4.1-mini,
   the simulated owner qwen) lists the capabilities the OWNER stated, at the level of a release-note
   entry, at most 25, with a `kind` (feature or constraint) and a quote.
2. **Quotes verified in code.** An entry is kept only if its quote (5 words or more) is verbatim in
   the owner's own messages (`quoteIsVerbatim`, the product's own check); the same statement listed
   twice counts once; constraints (stack, policy, retention) are reported but not part of recall.
   This costs recall of statements the extractor paraphrased, equally in every set.
3. **Coverage, deterministic first.** A requirement covers a capability outright when its evidence
   quote is the capability's quote or two thirds of the capability's words are in its title and
   acceptance; a capability that shares a fifth of its words with nothing is uncovered; the middle
   goes to a second call of the same cheap model, which may only name one of the three best
   candidates. Three cases are reported: the final FR list, anywhere in the specification (the FR
   list plus every other recorded field and the requirements curation rerouted), and the FRs as
   recorded before curation (isolates capture from merging).
4. **Precision.** The share of requirements the owner's words support (own evidence verbatim, or
   matched to a stated capability). A lower bound: an owner who answered "yes" to a proposal has no
   sentence of their own to quote.
5. **Intervals.** Wilson 95% on pooled items (too narrow: items of one interview are not
   independent) and a percentile bootstrap over interviews (honest, and wide at three per persona).

Same transcripts and facts as the earlier measurement, `evals/measurements/2026-10/`; results in
`*/recall6.json`, answers cached by prompt in `*/.recall-cache` so every table is recomputable at
zero cost. About 0.55 USD in all for the method, including four iterations that are not reported
(see "What went wrong with the method").

How to read it:

- **No loss.** Recall of what the owner said, on the same footing, did not fall: 62% to 85-87% on the
  FR list, 91% to 95-96% anywhere in the spec. The fresh interviews are different transcripts, so
  this is not a paired comparison; but it is recall against each transcript's own owner statements,
  and the interview that records fewer, distinct requirements covers more of them. The bootstrap
  intervals overlap at the 6 of 6 level (before 41-80, fresh 74-94 for the final set) and the
  direction is stable across three generations of the fresh code; "not worse" is shown, "better" is
  suggestive.
- **The earlier lists were padded.** Only 26% of their requirements are supported by an owner quote
  or a stated capability, against 98-100% now. Reading them agrees: `before/technical-1` lists 17,
  of which "require a patient to be selected before saving a note" appears as FR-3, FR-5 and FR-14,
  "edit history" as FR-7, FR-10 and FR-17: about 7 distinct capabilities. The stated-capability
  share that "did not improve" (36% to 39%) was the judge model's own list and coverage, checked
  nowhere; the same transcripts scored by this method give 91% against 95%.
- **Where the FR list misses things, the specification mostly has them elsewhere.** The gap between
  "FR list" and "anywhere" is stack and integration choices the technical persona stated
  ("authenticate via Azure AD SSO", "Terraform", "FHIR R4"): recorded under `technical`,
  `integrations`, `security`, as D-242 intends. The technical persona's FR list recall of 41% before
  is that, not lost requirements.
- **Curation loses little.** Capture to final (FRs as recorded against FR list): 67% to 60-67% in the
  replays, 82% to 82% and 89% to 87% in the fresh sets. The item-level losses found by hand were
  rollout and test-plan statements ("test with 20 nurses in a sandbox") rerouted to rules by design,
  stack choices in the technical persona, and, in one fresh interview, a launch-from-Epic-button
  capability merged under "cancel and return to Epic".
- **The 8 to 20 target was measuring the padding.** The fresh interviews hold 5 to 10 requirements
  (median 8 on this change, 7 before; generation varies by one or two between runs of the same
  facts). See "Targets" below.

### Hand validation of the matcher

Fourteen covered and ten uncovered capabilities of the `before` and `after-grouped` sets, read by
hand: 11 of 14 covered were correct, 3 were weak (a title-only match, "auto-save every 30 seconds"
under "patient note entry"; a toast under a received badge; "one clean entry per note" under a
finance import); all 10 uncovered were genuinely not in the FR list (stack decisions, a platform
statement, a technical integration). So recall is an upper bound by about 15 points, the same
direction in every set; the comparison between sets is what carries weight. One reader, 24 items.

## The over-merge, and what was changed

Reading the merges of the final fresh interviews (`merges` in the measurement; the facts hold the
merged titles) found the grouped pass (D-290) putting under one title requirements that a tester
would test separately:

- "A nurse taps a patient and sees a blank note", "logs in and sees the patient list with bed
  numbers" and "opens a patient's full note history from a View History button" under one FR.
- "Grey Pending badge", "night nurse does a manual check after 5 minutes pending" and "system logs a
  failure in Azure Monitor after 5 minutes pending" under one FR.
- "Nurse launches the tool from an Epic button" under "cancel without saving and return to Epic".

A merged requirement keeps only the survivor's title; the others' acceptance criteria are appended,
but their titles were not, so the document no longer named the capability. By a no-model count
(`mergedAway`, merged titles with fewer than 60% of their words in the survivor) that is 3 of 33
merges in the fresh set and 4 of 60 in the replay: small, and in the direction of loss. Changed:

| Where                              | Change                                                                                                                                                                                                                 |
| ---------------------------------- | ---------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| `requirement_merge@3`              | Default reversed: behavior of its own (a state, a log entry, an alert, a history or list view) stays apart, "when unsure leave it out", three examples taken from the over-merges above. `@2` retired in the pin file  |
| `mergeGroup` (`spec/quality.ts`)   | A merged title the survivor's words do not hold (under 80%, two or more words missing) is kept as an acceptance criterion, a check a tester can run; a title that is a restatement or one missing word is not repeated |
| `evals/src/recall.ts` `mergedAway` | The no-model count above, in the live script's per-interview details                                                                                                                                                   |

Measured effect: not shown. On the same facts the model still groups the same things (`@3` against
`@2` produced the same over-merges on mistral-small), and the spec-generation model rewrites
acceptance criteria and may drop an appended title. The no-model count was 3 of 33 before and 6 of 32
after with the guard (generation variance on a count this small; the survivor's words are rewritten),
the model-judged FR list recall did not move (87% against 85%, 82% against 82% as recorded). The
guard is kept because it is cheap, tested, and in the safe direction; this note does not claim it as
an improvement. A stronger model on the merge route is the lever that was not tried.

## What went wrong with the method (so it is not repeated)

1. The first matcher (`gpt-4.1-nano`) and a permissive extractor produced 60 to 170 "capabilities"
   an interview, mostly details, and recall of 70-90% that said little. Capping the extractor at
   release-note granularity and a strict `feature`/`constraint` kind brought it to 9 to 25.
2. An id with a colon (`S:rules/x`) was cut at the colon by the model-answer parser, so
   "anywhere in the spec" first came out lower than the FR list alone. Anywhere is now the union.
3. Merged titles were first resolved back into the survivor's text for matching, which hid the loss
   the merge causes; the document does not carry them, so they are not read.
4. The extractor returned nothing usable for one or two interviews a run (output cut at the token
   limit; a failed reply was cached as null until fixed). A cut list is salvaged to its last complete
   entry and a reply that is not JSON is never cached. The count of verified statements still varies
   by 10 to 15% between runs of the same transcript (a provider that is not deterministic at
   temperature 0); percentages moved by a few points, conclusions did not.

## Not done

- The duplicate judge (`spec-quality.live.ts`) was not re-run on this change; its previous
  duplicate rate (11%) is the latest.
- Three interviews a persona. The technical persona's interval is wide; a larger set is the next
  measurement, not more prompts.
- Recall counts only what the owner said in their own words with a verifiable quote. A capability the
  owner accepted by saying "yes" to a proposal is not in the denominator, and an interviewer that
  proposed too much would not be penalized here; the read-back (D-244) and `needsConfirmation` are
  the control for that, and are measured elsewhere.
- The matcher is a cheap model checked by one reader on 24 items; recall is an upper bound by about
  15 points.
- Run 2 happened after the fixes, and the defects are visible in run 1 only: one run each. The
  duplicate ids were found in 2 of the 2 specifications of run 1 and in none of 2 of run 2, a sample
  and not a rate; the unit and database tests are what show the collision resolved.
